## Additional authoritative and primary anchors -- selected verification highlights

- **Essiac/Flor Essence (NCI PDQ):** **Verified**. "No controlled data are available from human studies to suggest that Essiac or Flor Essence are effective in the treatment of patients with cancer." Additionally, some evidence suggests Flor Essence may **increase** tumor formation in an animal model of breast cancer -- a safety signal, not merely an absence-of-benefit finding.
- **Graviola/soursop (MSK monograph):** **Verified**. Human anticancer evidence is lacking; concentrated leaf/seed/bark extracts contain higher concentrations of annonacin, an acetogenin linked to atypical Parkinsonism with very high long-term consumption -- a real neurologic safety signal distinguished by plant part and preparation, not a blanket statement about the fruit.
- **Cochrane CD007731 (Ganoderma lucidum/reishi):** **Verified**. Latest update (pub3, 2016): 5 RCTs, 373 subjects, all Chinese-population trials, small and methodologically limited; does not support first-line cancer treatment use. The proposal brief's supplied URL pointed to the 2012 pub2 version; pub3 (2016) is cited here as the current version.
- **ASCO-SIO 2024 fatigue guideline (PMID 38754041):** **Verified**. American ginseng is a conditional recommendation, low-quality evidence, specifically for adults on active treatment (distinguished within the same guideline from post-treatment survivorship interventions). Evidence base: 113 RCTs across all fatigue interventions studied.
- **McCulloch et al. 2006 (Astragalus + platinum chemotherapy, NSCLC):** **Verified**. 34 RCTs, 2815 patients; RR 0.67 for death at 12 months and RR 1.34 for tumor response with added Astragalus-based herbs -- but the meta-analysis's own authors flag poor quality in several included studies and call for confirmation in well-designed trials. Not a standalone tumor-control recommendation.
- **Frass et al. 2020 NSCLC retraction (cross-reference from the sibling homeopathy proposal):** Not applicable here -- this is a homeopathy paper, kept in the separate `homeopathy-oncology-evidence` proposal, not imported into this module. Mentioned only to note that "Frass" and "Frenkel" recur as investigator names across multiple integrative-oncology literatures reviewed across this repository's sibling proposals; no TCM-specific integrity concern was identified for the sources cited in this module.

## Deliberate non-imports

- **PMC6536969's and BioRes Scientia's broad benefit statements** are not imported as clinical conclusions -- both are registered `discovery_only`/`narrative_review`, structurally incapable of supporting anything beyond `research_only`/`insufficient_evidence` per the validator's non-efficacy-source-type rule.
- **The Memorial Healthcare System page's dose-like and bioavailability-enhancement advice** (piperine-pairing instructions, one-clove-per-day dosing, allicin-formation timing) is not imported into any card or diet template; see `card-culinary-turmeric-curcumin-nonequivalence`, `card-culinary-garlic-nonequivalence`.
- **PMC5073821's reported items** (soursop, wheatgrass, saffron, aloe, garlic, ginger, beetroot, carrot, papaya, mixed juices) are not converted into a breast/prostate/colorectal-cancer diet or an efficacy mapping; see `card-pmc5073821-observational-use-survey-record` and `card-graviola-soursop-caribbean-context`.
- **No herb-to-cancer mapping was invented** from a commercial, narrative, conference, or encyclopedia source alone; where no qualifying human evidence exists, the corresponding card is `blocked_unsupported`, `preclinical_research_only`, or `out_of_scope_adjacent` rather than filled in.
