### Methods, as stated on the handout

A narrative (not systematic) literature selection: PubMed searches in September 2016 ("homeopathy," "cancer," "treatment," free full text, 2012+) and January 2017 ("adverse effects" + several cancer types), plus supplemental hand searches, yielding 45 total included articles (33 from the primary search, 10 from hand searches, 2 from a secondary search) per the handout's own PRISMA-style flow figure. This is a narrative literature selection for a conference talk, not a systematic review with a documented risk-of-bias framework -- treated accordingly as `conference_handout`, tier context-only.

### Content, separated by category

**Traditional theory / terminology:** the handout defines dilution notation (X, C, CH, M, CM, MT) as bibliographic convention, which this proposal reuses the same way -- as study metadata, never as dosing instruction.

**"Curative" claims (the handout's own framing, not this proposal's):**
- The Prasanta Banerji Homeopathic Research Foundation (PBHRF) case series: ~17,324-19,317 cancer patients, 1990-2005, reported 19% complete/21% partial regression or stabilization (Frenkel 2015). The handout **itself states these outcomes "have not been replicated in other trials"** and attributes this partly to "differences in homeopathic treatments' production." The US National Cancer Institute's "Best Case Series" review of related material found supportive cases across several tumor types but, per the handout, "the actual treatments used are not provided" -- i.e., unverifiable by formulation. Registered as context for `card-homeopathy-general-any-cancer`, `blocked_unsupported`.
- A retrospective series of 246 gallbladder/liver/lung/oral/pancreatic/stomach cancer patients from the Critical Cancer Management Research Centre & Clinic (Kolkata), including a described case where Psorinum-6x (derived, per the handout, from scabies-blister fluid) achieved "complete response in metastatic adenocarcinoma of the gallbladder... as the sole anti-cancer treatment... for 2 years, all without adverse effects." **This is the single most safety-relevant claim in the handout** -- an uncontrolled, single-center, retrospective series describing homeopathy as sole treatment for a metastatic solid tumor with a "no adverse effects" claim that cannot be independently verified from this source. Modeled as its own card, `card-psorinum-sole-treatment-case-series`, state `avoid`.
- Multiple in-vitro and in-vivo (mouse/rat) studies reporting apoptotic/cytotoxic effects of ultramolecular *Conium*, *Thuja*, *Phytolacca*, *Carcinosin*, *Ruta*, *Lycopodium*, *Sulphur*, *Serenoa*, and others against cancer cell lines and murine tumor models -- all preclinical, modeled as `research_only` and structurally barred by the validator from reaching `clinician_discussion_only`.
- A table (cited to "Kalra, 2016") reporting "Cure 14%, Prevented 41%, Palliated 14%, Improved 31%" outcome percentages by remedy (Calc-Carb, Carcinosin, Conium M, Phytolacca, Thuja, "Biochemical salt," Others) for breast cancer subtypes. This table's percentages are reproduced **nowhere** in this proposal's evidence cards -- presenting cure/prevention percentages sourced from an unverified table is exactly the pattern this module exists to block, and the underlying primary source was not independently located in this pass.

**"Concurrent" claims:** NK-cell chemosensitization background (largely conventional immunology, not homeopathy-specific) plus a 3-month trial of complex Heel-type products (Ubichinon Compositum, Glyoxal Compositum, Katalysatoren, Traumeel Coenzyme Compositum) reporting increased NK cytotoxic activity -- a fixed multi-ingredient complex product result, not evidence for classical homeopathy.

**"Supportive" claims:** Nux vomica/Radium bromatum/Belladonna/Lachesis for general chemo adverse effects; Calendula and Traumeel for radiodermatitis/stomatitis; the Cocculine CINV null RCT (Pérol et al.); the menopausal-symptom trials for endocrine-therapy patients; the 410-patient Austrian Frass et al. 2015 RCT (**the handout itself discloses** that constipation/diarrhea/nausea-vomiting were not significantly improved, that only treatment responders were analyzed rather than intent-to-treat, and that concurrent access to other integrative treatments and flexible remedy selection "preclude study replication and transfer to clinical practice" -- direct quotes from the handout's own limitations bullet); and hetero-isotherapy (using a dilution of the substance that caused an adverse reaction) for chemo-drug hand-foot syndrome and folliculitis, an approach with no established mechanism and only small/case-level support per the handout.

**Adverse effects (the handout's most valuable section for this proposal):**
- Direct statement: "Nonuse of conventional treatment due to homeopathy use can contribute to disease progression, which is considered an adverse effect of homeopathy... Acute lymphatic leukemia is a malignant condition for which this has occurred" (cited to Posadzki et al., 2012). This is a documented **lethal-potential harm precedent** for treatment substitution and is used directly in `card-metastatic-breast-substitution-case`'s and `card-psorinum-sole-treatment-case-series`'s safety framing.
- "More than 74 integrative medicine preparations contained toxic organic substances, excessive heavy metals, or had microbial contaminants" (Ben-Arye et al., 2012) -- product-quality/contamination risk, cited above.
- *Aristolochia clematitis* (aristolochic acid -- a recognized human carcinogen and nephrotoxin, banned in multiple nations) shown inhibiting DNA synthesis in human hepatoma cells in vitro; kava (hepatotoxic, banned in several nations) and comfrey (hepatotoxic, "should be avoided") both flagged by the handout itself as toxic source substances used in some homeopathic/herbal-adjacent preparations.
- FDA action against Hyland's Teething Tablets (belladonna) from 2010: purported ultra-low belladonna content but documented infant toxicity (agitation, constipation, respiratory/voiding difficulty, lethargy, seizures, somnolence), leading pharmacies to voluntarily stop selling the product -- the clearest available real-world precedent for **incorrect-dilution/manufacturing-quality risk**, independent of any question about homeopathy's mechanism.
- A German pediatric-oncology survey (Gottschling et al., 2014; n=457): 62% used an integrative practitioner without oncologist direction, and adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, symptom aggravation requiring medical intervention) from homeopathy/phytotherapy were reported in a subset -- supports this proposal's `standard_care_statement` emphasis on disclosure to the oncology team.

**Author's own advocacy (kept separate from the evidence, per this proposal's tradition/evidence separation rule):** the handout's conclusion states a goal "to globally increase patients' homeopathy use, lessening geographical variances" and that positive RCTs "if followed... may secure a position [for homeopathy] in curative cancer treatment" (explicitly future-conditional, not a current efficacy claim). This is the author's stated advocacy position, not a evidence finding, and is recorded here for transparency rather than treated as a source of clinical fact.

### Investigator-concentration finding

Two names recur across both the handout's most-cited "positive" material and this pass's independent verification, which matters for how much weight the broader literature can bear:

- **Michael Frass** is the lead author of both the 410-patient 2015 Austrian RCT (cited throughout the handout's "Supportive Cancer Therapy" section, itself carrying disclosed responder-only/non-ITT bias) **and** the since-retracted 2020 NSCLC survival paper (data falsification finding). The 2015 paper is not itself retracted or accused of misconduct, but a documented integrity finding against the same investigator's later, higher-stakes trial is a legitimate reason for added scrutiny and independent replication before any certainty upgrade on the 2015 result -- recorded explicitly in `card-global-wellbeing-fatigue-qol`'s limitations.
- **Moshe Frenkel** is both a repeatedly-cited review author throughout the handout (Frenkel 2015 appears as the citation for the PBHRF claim, the adverse-effects systematic review, several supportive-care claims, and more) **and** the principal investigator of the withdrawn NCT02190539 Banerji-protocol trial. This is not an integrity concern, but it does mean a meaningful fraction of the handout's positive framing traces back to a small number of overlapping investigators and reviewers rather than independent groups -- worth noting for anyone assessing how much independent confirmation this literature actually has.
